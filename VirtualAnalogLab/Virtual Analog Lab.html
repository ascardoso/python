Virtual Analog Lab

A sawtooth oscillator into a four-stage ladder filter, calculated sample by sample in your browser. Flip the switches below while a note plays and watch what changes in the spectrum.

The naive saw is the textbook formula. Its high harmonics fold back below the Nyquist limit as inharmonic noise.

The ladder bends each stage with tanh, like transistors. At full resonance it sings on its own, and drive thickens it.

PlayTap keys, or use A W S E D F T G Y H U J K on your keyboard.

Spectrum40 Hz to 20 kHz, log scale. Grass between the harmonics is aliasing.

WaveformFlat tops mean the stages are saturating.

What to try

1. Hold a low note and switch between naive and PolyBLEP: the noise floor between harmonics drops.
2. Set resonance to 4.2 with Ladder on and pull the cutoff down: the filter rings at its own pitch with nothing feeding it.
3. Switch to Ideal: resonance stops at 3.9 to stay stable, drive no longer matters, and the waveform never flattens.
Caveat: this filter reads its feedback from the previous sample (with 2× oversampling to soften the error). Production models solve that loop exactly.
