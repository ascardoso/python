Brain Music Live

Reads an OpenEEG-style board (P2 protocol), turns the signal into five band strengths measured against your own baseline, and plays them as instruments. It is for fun and exploration, not a medical or diagnostic tool.

Not connected.

Needs Chrome or Edge on a computer, opened from a file or a regular web page. Keep the board on battery and your laptop unplugged while electrodes are on you. Wait about 30 seconds after connecting for the signal to settle.

MIDI out

Each band uses its own MIDI channel (1 to 5). Delta and theta are held pads whose volume is sent as CC11. Alpha, beta and gamma send notes. The SAM2695 route needs a second USB serial adapter at 3.3 V logic, with its TX wired to the chip's RX and a shared ground. Check the SAM2695 datasheet for its exact voltage and pin details before wiring.

Alpha (eyes closed) is the band most likely to respond clearly. Gamma at the scalp is weak and often muscle noise, so treat it as texture. When the signal is flagged bad (clipping or large movement), the music holds its last clean values.
